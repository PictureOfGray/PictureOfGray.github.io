Unit Digests

Omicron Galaxy

Greetings, Lance Leaders,

According to old DMI records, there was never a Clan Jade Falcon Omicron Galaxy. I guess that needs updating now. My staff and I believe Omicron Galaxy was stood up as a side effect of the Falcon desant.

From the Stormhammers' BattleROM footage, we've identified the livery of Gyrfalcon, Alpha, and Zeta Galaxies. Together, the three ravaged Prefecture IX and have made incursions into Prefecture VIII. Eventually, Clan Jade Falcon carved out a foothold in Republic space — the desant. However, many of their warriors are allegedly green, dissident, or dishonorable. These Jade Falcons also need to hold an area with limited supplies and reinforcements.

Therefore, we're fairly confident Omicron Galaxy was cobbled together to increase tactical and strategic flexibility. A fourth Galaxy allows the other Galaxy Commanders to maintain their grip on the desant while still sending out troops to raid for supplies or sow fear. And, of course, a new Galaxy provides an opportunity to weed out the weak and blood some veterans.

A crucial piece of evidence is Omicron Galaxy's smaller size compared to its peers. If Omicron is one big melting pot for broken commands, bondsmen, old solahma troops, and fresh sibkos, then that would explain why it didn't exist until after the Battle of Skye.

Regardless of their origin, Omicron Galaxy is here on Zollikofen. They were likely drawn here because of its old BattleMech cache and its strategic location close to Terra. Clan culture holds a pseudospiritual belief that the first clan to take Terra shall become ilClan, the Clan of Clans, and lead humanity to a golden age under the aegis of a new Star League. Of course, we live on Terra, and if these ruthless Falcons get there, it'll be quite literally Hell on Earth.

Each Galaxy is made of several Clusters, which are in turn made up of several Stars, which are made up of 5 BattleMechs (or a Point of Elementals, or two vehicles). These are led by a Galaxy Commander, Star Colonels, and Star Commanders (respectively).

We haven't gotten much intel yet on the lower ranks of leadership, but Galaxy Commander Urakov Hazen is well-known to us (relatively speaking, at least). His Hazen Bloodname suggests he probably follows the Mongol Doctrine, the same line of thinking that led to nukes being launched on Skye, courtesy of the insane Galaxy Commander Malvina Hazen. Expect brutal fighting, with no quarter taken or given. Urakov is likely the most dangerous person on Zollikofen - strong, ambitious, and driven. This world will be his Rubicon.

All Clanners, however, have a sense of honor. For example, none of them will engage in melee until their opponent does it first. Additionally, their zellbrigen will dictate what assets they bid away for a fair fight. However, this new generation of Jade Falcons typically disregards zellbrigen toward Spheroids like us, though it might still be a useful asset. Knight Beneventi wields enough respect to challenge Urakov to a one-on-one Trial of Possession for all of Zollikofen. Our hopes ride with the Knight - but it never hurts to have a backup plan.

The Winged Keshik

Greetings, Lance Leaders,

As you may already know, the Keshik is the traditional honor guard of a Khan or Galaxy Commander. They are typically the best of the best, and sworn to stay near their charge and defend them with their lives. Each Keshik ranges in size, but is usually around a Cluster in strength and commanded by a Star Colonel. The Winged Keshik's leader is Star Colonel Casia Pryde, and is also Urakov Hazen's right-hand.

Urakov, however, has taken a different approach to his guard. The Winged Keshik is still present wherever their Commander goes, but their warriors are also seeded throughout Omicron. Scouts and survivors report 'Mechs belonging to the Winged Keshik can be seen working with frontline Stars, and sometimes even commanding them. Why they do this is currently unknown, but my team has a few theories, ranked from most probable to least:

  1. Urakov wants to keep his Keshik honed and inspire other warriors to their greatness.
  2. Urakov wants eyes and ears throughout Omicron to guard against dissension.
  3. The Winged Keshik's presence is a fear tactic.
  4. Members of the Winged Keshik are being deployed for leadership experience that will come into play when Urakov is gone.
  5. Omicron Galaxy is small enough to warrant regular deployment of the Winged Keshik.

Whatever the case, watch for the Winged Keshik's BattleMechs. You can identify them by their purple-and-khaki livery and their tendency to use 'Mechs named after birds. Failing that, they're often seen commanding Stars or executing bold plans. We advise you to always keep your head on a swivel when they're around. Good luck out there, MechWarriors.

An Abridged History of the Clans

Greetings, Lance Leaders

Lt. Col. Sissako noted that it always helps to know your history before entering a stressful environment. While I believe she was referring to an interview or boardroom deal, it can still help when divining an enemy's direction and diction. For your convenience, I've tasked Zollikofen's most tired intern with writing a report detailing how the Clans and the Jade Falcons ended up here.

The Clans' history begins with that of the first Star League. The 2770s were the twilight of the Star League. The aftermath of the Amaris Civil War shone a harsh light on the failure of humanity's greatest experiment, and the Great Houses of the Inner Sphere began carving up the Terran Hegemony (where our current Republic is). Commanding General Aleksandr Kerensky was the closest the shattered Star League bureaucracy had to a leader, and he spent most of his time trying to keep the House Lords from abandoning it. He was not successful.

And so, Kerensky began to make plans. The Star League Defense Force was the largest and most powerful in the Inner Sphere, and any alliance with one House or another would spread untold amounts of death and destruction – a fate he desperately wanted to avoid after thirteen years of civil war. In the shadows of his public efforts, Keresnky organized a contingency plan to leave the Inner Sphere altogether: Operation EXODUS. On 8 July, 2784, the Exodus Fleet formed and began the single greatest pilgrimage in human history.

Their destination was simple: anywhere but here. Kerensky's Exodus Fleet traveled past the Inner Sphere, past the Periphery, past the Deep Periphery, and into unknown deep space. After two arduous, long years, the Exodus Fleet found the Pentagon Worlds: Circe, Arcadia, Babylon, Bagda, and Eden. History's greatest military turned their swords into plowshares.

However, the new settlers still originated from the Inner Sphere, and the centuries of distrust associated with the cultures of old began to rear their head. In 2801, the Pentagon Civil War had begun, as different disgruntled groups declared independence and turned on their neighbors. Horrified by the carnage and determined to maintain his father's legacy, Nicholas Kerensky and his followers formed a Second Exodus and ventured deeper into the unknown.

They settled on Strana Mechty and the rest of the Kerensky Cluster. From there, the young Kerensky forged a new fate for his followers. Drawing on history and his own somewhat mad ramblings, he created the Clans: a confederation of hypermilitaristic, honor-bound, caste-based states. Twenty Clans were formed from the warriors of the Star League-in-Exile, each ruled by a Khan and a saKhan (with Kerensky as IlKhan). Citizens were separated into strict castes, with the Warrior Caste ruling. A brief description of each is as follows:

  • Blood Spirit, the esprit de Clan
  • Burrock, the sneaky Clan
  • Cloud Cobra, the religious Clan
  • Coyote, the science Clan
  • Sea Fox/Diamond Shark, the merchant Clan
  • Fire Mandril, the Clan of clans
  • Ghost Bear, the familial Clan
  • Goliath Scorpion, the Clan of history
  • Hell's Horses, the mixed-arms Clan
  • Ice Hellion, the speedy Clan
  • Jade Falcon, the prideful Clan
  • Mongoose, the aggressive Clan
  • Nova Cat, the Clan of prophecies
  • Smoke Jaguar, the tyrannical Clan
  • Snow Raven, the aerospace Clan
  • Star Adder, the strategic Clan
  • Steel Viper, the purist Clan
  • Widowmaker, the youngest Clan
  • Wolf, the pragmatic Clan
  • Wolverine, the not-named Clan

After careful preparation, the Clans executed Operation KLONDIKE and invaded the Pentagon Worlds. The campaign proved the Clans' primacy, who had gone so far as to adopt a different military structure than their Spheroid enemies. With the Star League-in-Exile under one banner, the Clans turned inwards to develop and quarrel in equal measure. Instead of full-scale war, the Clans believed in small-scale Trials (analogous to duels) with strict rules. This prevented widespread destruction and limited collateral damage. After IlKhan Kerensky's death in 2834, the Clans entered the Golden Century. Throughout this era, the Clans prospered. Iron wombs incubated genetically-modified generations of Warriors, the first battle armors were developed, the OmniMech was created, and many other advancements were made. Political beliefs also began to emerge, typically as answers to the greatest question in Clan society: What do we do about the Inner Sphere?

Since the beginning of Operation EXODUS, citizens held an underlying assumption that the Star League (and thus the Clans) would return to the Inner Sphere. The Hidden Hope Doctrine codified this. On one side of the aisle were the Crusaders, who believed it was their destiny to return to the Inner Sphere and bring the Great Houses to heel under the banner of a reborn Star League. Their primary detractors were the Wardens, who believed it was their destiny to return to the Inner Sphere and guard it from any corrupting influences or existential threats.

The gathering storm led to the start of the Political Century in 2947, which sparked fierce debate. In 3000, they reached a compromise: send a team of Clanners disguised as mercenaries to the Inner Sphere to gather intelligence before a formal invasion. This was seen as a triumph for the Wardens, who thought it would stall the invasion plans. Thus, the mysterious Wolf's Dragoons arrived in the Inner Sphere with lost technology and served every Great House.

However, in 3019, the Dragoons “went native” and cut contact with the Clan Homeworlds. Further outrage flared when the ComStar JumpShip the Outbound Light entered Clan space in 3048. Clan Smoke Jaguar, the fiercest and cruelest of the Clans, captured the vessel and presented them as invaders to the Clan Council. A vote was held, and the Clans began Trials to determine the finer points of the Clan Invasion. Four Clans were considered more than enough.

Meanwhile, the Inner Sphere had suffered through four Succession Wars and was finally rebuilding by the middle of the century. The Federated Suns and Lyran Commonwealth had formed the Federated Commonwealth, and the Free Rasalhague Republic had earned its independence and nestled itself between the Lyrans and the Draconis Combine in the galactic north. It saw twenty years of freedom. In 3050, Clans Wolf, Jade Falcon, Smoke Jaguar, and Ghost Bear invaded the galactic north in a vicious blitzkrieg. Planet after planet found themselves under sudden attack by unknown invaders, whose 'Mechs were faster, stronger, and longer-ranged than contemporary Spheroid machines. The opening waves of the Clan Invasion were so disastrous that ComStar initially believed the Clanners were aliens.

By 3051, the Inner Sphere was scrambling, and if it were not for the sudden death of the IlKhan and two key victories by the Federated Commonwealth and Draconis Combine, dozens of planets could have fallen. However, due to Clan rituals surrounding the election of IlKhan, the Inner Sphere had a year of peace. Thanks to the peacemaking efforts of Wolf's Dragoons, the Spheroids set aside their differences and mounted a unified defense. In response, the Clans brought in three reserve clans: the Diamond Sharks, the Nova Cats, and the Steel Vipers.

In 3052, the juggernaut had been slowed, but fighting still raged. ComStar, which was collaborating with the Clans, learned of their true objective: Terra itself. ComStar's world. Desperate to save themselves, they called for a Trial on Tukayyid to determine Terra's fate. The Clans underestimated their opponent, and ComStar won what many call “the greatest battle in history.” For the next 15 years, no Clan could go below the Tukayyid Line.

Peace was finally secured, but the Inner Sphere wanted a show of force to fully dissuade the Clanners. In the late 3050s and early 3060s, two campaigns, Operations BIRD DOG and BULLDOG, not only ejected Clan Smoke Jaguar from the Inner Sphere, but annihilated them in the Clan Homeworlds. The Great Refusal in April 3060 ended the Clan Invasion.

By 3067, with the end of the Tukayyid Truce and the beginning of the Jihad, a new divide had grown between Clans. Several of the Clans in the Clan Homeworlds, led by the Steel Vipers, began to resent the Spheroid Clans. They saw the Spheroid Clans as weak and tainted by the greed of the Great Houses. In 3071, the Steel Vipers and their alliances began the Wars of Reaving. They annihilated genetic lineages and even entire Clans. The code of honor holding back Clan warfare was cast off, and the Homeworlds saw their own Succession War. By the 3080s, only four Clans were stil in the Homeworlds: the Coyotes, the Star Adders, the Cloud Cobras, and a new Clan, the Stone Lions. Everyone else either left or were Annihilated. The survivors agreed to isolate themselves entirely. Their current status is unknown.

The Wars of Reaving led several Clans to flee to the Inner Sphere. The Nova Cats became a puppet of the Draconis Combine. The Ghost Bears merged with the remnants of the Free Rasalhague Republic. The Snow Ravens and Goliath Scorpions found refuge in the Periphery, and the Hell's Horses stole territory from the Wolves and Jade Falcons. The Diamond Sharks became the Sea Foxes and disseminated their wares throughout the Sphere. Though the Clans' militant natures chafed under the era of Pax Republicana, they rebuilt themselves as every other nation did.

The opening years of this Dark Age, and the end of the Republic's good years, saw several changes to the Clans. The Republic found the Ghost Bear Dominion to be agreeable allies, while the Sea Foxes found ample buyers in the prefectures. Additionally, the Jade Falcons split between those who wanted to renew their ancestors' invasion and those who did not. Last year, the Falcons invaded Prefecture IX in a desant, nearly burning Skye to the ground. Now, they are continuing their advance, and rumour has it the Wolves may also be looking towards Terra. Let us pray we only need to clip the Falcons' wings.
